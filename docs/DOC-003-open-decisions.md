# DOC-003 Open decisions and release blockers

Every question only the owner can answer is listed here, open or closed. Nothing in code or documents guesses an answer to an open decision. A decision is closed only with the owner's answer in the conversation, recorded as "(owner, YYYY-MM-DD)", and carried into the items it affects in the same change.

`Blocks:` says what cannot happen while the decision is open:

- `submission`: the hackathon submission (November 27, 2026, 11:59 PM ET) cannot be completed.
- `real-world use`: the prototype must not be used with real people, medicines or aircraft.
- `none`: improves the product but blocks nothing.

Options: (a) "Not ready" is retired; a blocking issue makes the indicator "No go" and is listed there and in the draft paperwork, and the meter shows its percentage as usual; (b) as (a), and the meter also shows a "Not ready" label while any blocking issue is open; (c) the meter is capped at a set value while any blocking issue is open (the value would be the owner's). Claude recommends (a): one indicator, one word. Raised in iteration 1 (2026-11-12); TC-105 waits on it.

### OD-053 WHO's exceptions to the one-year shelf-life minimum
- Status: Closed
- Blocks: none
- Affects: SYS-102
- Decision: Option (a): neither exception applies in this build; every lot needs 365 days at arrival (owner, 2026-11-13). Where six months is used, as in the second exception, it is 183 days (owner, 2026-11-13). Both exceptions are to be revisited in the build-out after the hackathon, beyond the MVP. Recorded in ADR-022.

The WHO medicine-donation guidelines (revised 2010, Article 6), read on 2026-11-12, ask for at least one year of shelf life after arrival, and allow two exceptions: (1) direct donations to a specific health facility whose staff know the shelf life is limited and can use the stock before expiry; (2) medicines with a total shelf life under two years, of which at least one third, and at least six months, should remain. SYS-102 mentions lowering the minimum for direct donations by item category, but no category or value is set, and the second exception is not in the requirements. Until decided, neither exception applies, so every lot needs 365 days at arrival: the stricter reading. Linked to OD-002.

### OD-054 Lots from different manufacturers
- Status: Closed
- Blocks: none
- Affects: SYS-113, SYS-117, AIC-101, TC-107, TC-112, TC-127
- Decision: (1) Donor lines merge into one lot only when the manufacturer matches too; lines from different manufacturers stay separate lots. (2) Option (a): eligible lots of the same generic name, strength and dosage form count directly toward a floor item whoever made them, each lot shown with its manufacturer (owner, 2026-11-13). Recorded in ADR-024.

In the review of floor coverage (2026-11-13) the owner said that lots incidentally identical between manufacturers are not necessarily the same product: lots of one manufacturer from different donors can be the same lot, but different manufacturers never release the same lot. Two rules are affected, and each needs the owner's answer:

1. **Merging donor lines into one lot** (OD-011, TC-107): lines merge when lot number, generic name, strength, form and expiry all match. Proposed: the manufacturer must match too; lines from different manufacturers stay separate lots, even with the same lot number.
2. **Counting lots toward a floor item** (SYS-113, TC-112): today every eligible lot of the same generic name, strength and dosage form counts directly, whoever made it, each listed separately with its donor. Options: (a) keep counting them directly, each lot shown with its manufacturer; (b) count directly only lots of one manufacturer's product, and offer other manufacturers' lots like exchanges, counting only when the clinician accepts them and enters the amount covered; (c) count them directly, but the item needs review until the clinician confirms the manufacturers present.

Until decided, the code built so far counts as in option (a).

### OD-055 Whether a chosen comparable alternative counts toward the floor
- Status: Closed
- Blocks: none
- Affects: SYS-108, SYS-112, RC-105, HAZ-105
- Decision: Option (b): a comparable alternative the clinician chooses counts exactly the share of the target the clinician enters, as an exchange does, and the item shows that it is covered partly by a comparable alternative the clinician chose (owner, 2026-11-15). The owner noted that an injectable form would be an unusual choice; in this build injectables are outside scope and never offered (SYS-109). Recorded in ADR-026.

SYS-108 and ADR-003 say comparable alternatives (another medicine in the same class) are listed for the clinician and "added only when the clinician chooses them", but not whether a chosen alternative then counts toward the floor item's target, and so toward "Go". HAZ-105 (an alternative taken as equivalent) is rated critical. Options: (a) a chosen alternative never counts toward the floor: it is packed as a supplement (SYS-114), and the item stays short, so the indicator stays "No go" until the floor item itself is met; (b) it counts exactly the share of the target the clinician enters when choosing it, as an exchange does (ADR-011), and the item shows that it is covered partly by a comparable alternative the clinician chose; (c) it counts its full quantity. Until decided, the build follows (a), the more cautious. Claude recommends (b): it keeps the clinician's explicit choice and amount, the label stays visible, and the indicator can reflect a deliberate clinical decision; (c) is not recommended. Raised in iteration 1 (2026-11-15).
